# Distribution Playbook

## How to get the reporting form to affected populations — fast

**Goal:** Reach affected communities within **6 hours** of deployment (not 2-3 days like Venezuela).

---

## 🎯 Core Principle

> The best tool is useless if it doesn't reach the people who need it.

In Venezuela, the form reached Jewish communities but missed the broader affected population. Distribution was the #1 failure point. This playbook exists to fix that.

## 📡 Distribution Channels (Priority Order)

### Tier 1: Direct to Affected Population (First 6 hours)

1. **Local WhatsApp/Telegram groups** — The primary communication channel in Latin America and many disaster zones. Pre-identify groups in risk areas.

2. **Local media contacts** — TV, radio, and online news in the affected country. Radio is critical — it works without internet/power.

3. **Municipal/government social media** — Tag local emergency management accounts.

4. **Community leaders & influencers** — Local figures with large followings who can amplify the message.

### Tier 2: Institutional (6–24 hours)

5. **INSARAG** — Direct contact with our liaison. They can distribute through official SAR channels.

6. **OCHA (UN Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs)** — Coordinates international response.

7. **Red Cross / Red Crescent (IFRC)** — Present in almost every country.

8. **HOT (Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team)** — Tech-savvy volunteer network.

### Tier 3: Amplification (24–72 hours)

9. **X/Twitter** — Posts in local language, tag relevant accounts.

10. **Facebook groups** — Local community and disaster response groups.

11. **Reddit** — r/earthquake, country-specific subreddits.

12. **Diaspora communities** — Relevant but should NOT be the primary channel.

## 📋 Pre-Event Preparation

### Build Contact Database NOW (Before Any Event)

For each high-risk zone, maintain:

- [ ] **3+ local media contacts** (TV, radio, online)
- [ ] **5+ WhatsApp/Telegram community groups** (general population, not just diaspora)
- [ ] **2+ local government/emergency management contacts**
- [ ] **2+ local NGO contacts** (Red Cross chapter, etc.)
- [ ] **5+ social media influencers** with local following
- [ ] **INSARAG regional coordinator** contact

### Priority Risk Zones (Latin America)

Based on seismic activity and recent events:
- Venezuela (Mw 7.2, June 2026)
- Colombia (Mw 7.4, August 2026)
- Chile, Peru, Ecuador, Mexico, Guatemala, Haiti

## ⏱️ Timing Protocol

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| 0–1h | Deploy form |
| 1–2h | Send to Tier 1 contacts (local WhatsApp, media) |
| 2–6h | Activate Tier 2 (INSARAG, OCHA, Red Cross) |
| 6–24h | Tier 3 amplification (social media, diaspora) |
| 24–72h | Monitor, adjust messaging, expand reach |
| 72h+ | Transition from "missing" to "status update" mode |

## 📊 Distribution Metrics

Track for each event:
- Time from earthquake to first external share
- Number of unique form submissions per channel
- Geographic spread of reporters vs. affected area
- Conversion: form views → completed submissions

---

## ⚠️ Lessons from Venezuela (June 2026)

| What went wrong | Fix |
|----------------|-----|
| Form arrived 2-3 days late | Pre-built template, deploy in < 2h |
| Reached Jewish communities only | Tier 1 = local population, not diaspora |
| No media coverage | Pre-establish media contacts |
| No institutional distribution | Pursue documented INSARAG/OCHA relationships before the next event |
| Single language assumed | Multilingual from day 1 |
