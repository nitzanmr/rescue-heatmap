# Lessons Learned: Colombia Earthquake, August 2026

**Event:** M7.4 earthquake
**Date:** August 10, 2026
**Time:** 07:34 local (12:34 UTC)
**Location:** 5 km south of San José del Palmar, Chocó department, western Colombia
**Depth:** ~110 km (deep — reduced surface damage compared to shallow quakes)
**Aftershock:** M5.0 recorded shortly after
**Tsunami:** No warning issued
**USGS PAGER:** Orange (high likelihood of casualties and damage)

## Initial Assessment (Day 1)

- **Casualties:** 70-80+ reported dead *(early media reports, rising)*
- **Injuries:** 69 reported in Chocó alone, more across other departments *(unverified)*
- **Damage:** Buildings collapsed in major cities — Cali, Pereira, Bogotá, Medellín all felt strong shaking
- **Infrastructure:** Power, water, communications disrupted; airport operations affected
- **Trapped:** Confirmed reports of people trapped under rubble, rescue ongoing

## Context

This earthquake occurred just ~6 weeks after the Venezuela M7.2 earthquake — the second major seismic event in the region in quick succession.

Key differences from Venezuela:
- **Deeper earthquake** (110 km vs. shallow) — damage more geographically spread but less concentrated
- **Colombia has stronger infrastructure** and more developed emergency systems than Venezuela
- **UNGRD** (Colombia's disaster management agency) is a functional institution

## What We Had Ready

- Lessons learned from Venezuela documented
- Reporting form concept reportedly reviewed by INSARAG *(unverified — see Venezuela lessons)*
- Team awareness and willingness to act
- Distribution playbook drafted

## What We Did NOT Have Ready

- [ ] Deployed rescue-heatmap MVP
- [ ] Pre-established contacts in Colombia
- [x] GitHub repo set up *(created August 10, 2026 — during the event, not before)*
- [ ] Automated deployment pipeline

## Lessons

> *To be updated as the event unfolds and response is evaluated*

1. **6 weeks was not enough** — Despite clear intention after Venezuela, the MVP was not built. Need dedicated development sprints, not just plans.

2. **Two events in one region** — Latin America's seismic activity means we should prepare for clusters, not isolated events.

3. **The 72-hour window** — Once again, the critical window is ticking while we organize. Pre-built tools would have allowed immediate response.
